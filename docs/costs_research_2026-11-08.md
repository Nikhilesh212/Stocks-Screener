# NSE cash equity delivery: round-trip costs and capital-gains tax (resident individual)

**Verification date: 2026-11-08.** Rates are those in force on this date. "Official" means a government, regulator, exchange or depository source (Income Tax Department, indiabudget.gov.in, egazette, SEBI, NSE, BSE, CDSL, NSDL). "Secondary" means a broker or commentary page. Statute sections are given for both the Income-tax Act, 1961 (1961 Act) and the Income-tax Act, 2025 (2025 Act, Act No. 30 of 2025, in force from 1 April 2026 per s.1(3)). The Finance Act, 2026 received Presidential assent on 30 March 2026.

## Notes per item

**1. STT.** The NSE circular NSE/FATAX/73524 (31 Mar 2026) lists every STT rate in force from 1 April 2026. Delivery buy and sell are 0.1% each, intraday sell is 0.025%, and all three are marked "No Change". The NSE investor page, updated 17 Apr 2026, agrees. One secondary search summary claimed delivery STT is "0.15%". That is wrong; disregard it. The 2013 start date for 0.1% is from memory and was not re-checked.

**2. Exchange transaction charges.**
- The total NSE charge has been Rs 307 per crore per side since 1 Oct 2024. From 1 Mar 2026 the split changed: almost all of it is now the transaction charge, with only Rs 0.01 going to IPFT. Zerodha shows this as "NSE 0.00307%". Groww still shows the old split, 0.00297% plus a separate IPFT of 0.0001%. The total is the same either way.
- Secondary sources disagree on the Sep 2024 circular number (NSE/FA/64232 or 64323). The Feb 2026 NSE circular cites NSE/FA/64323 dated 27 Sep 2024. I could not download the 64323 PDF (404).
- Before 1 Oct 2024 NSE used monthly-volume slabs, so retail rates of 0.00322% to 0.00325% appear in secondary sources.
- BSE: the master circular (PDF created 30 Apr 2026) gives Rs 375 per crore for A, B and other non-exclusive scrips, and Rs 275 for exclusive ones. The group table is partly an image. Secondary sources (a trader forum) say BSE cash charges did not change on 1 Oct 2024. Zerodha's page also lists much higher BSE charges for X, XT, Z, ZP, SS and ST groups (Rs 10,000 to Rs 1,00,000 per crore). I did not find an official BSE notice newer than 2022 that changes the A/B rate.
- No NSE cash-market charge revision after 1 Mar 2026 was found as of 2026-11-08.

**3. SEBI turnover fee.**
- The NSE page (updated 17 Apr 2026) shows 0.0001%, i.e. Rs 10 per crore, for non-debt securities.
- The SEBI Board memo of July 2020 shows Rs 10 as the "existing" fee and proposes a 50% cut for Jun 2020 to Mar 2021. That the cut was implemented is confirmed only by secondary sources. The Rs 15 to Rs 10 cut was approved by the SEBI Board on 1 Mar 2019 and notified on 22 Mar 2019. The CSE notice gives the effective date as 1 Apr 2019 (or 3 Apr 2019).
- The SEBI (Stock Brokers) Regulations, 2026 (notified 7 Jan 2026) replaced the 1992 regulations. I did not read their fee schedule directly. A secondary source says the fee stays at Rs 10.
- Zerodha's page source contains a hidden (commented-out) note saying "SEBI charges will be reduced to Rs 15 per crore". It is not displayed on the page and appears stale.

**4. Stamp duty.** Rates are from the NSE page. Stamp duty is levied only on the buyer and collected by clearing corporations under the Indian Stamp Act as amended by the Finance Act 2019. It took effect on 1 Jul 2020 after two deferrals (originally 9 Jan 2020, then 1 Apr 2020). I did not open the Department of Revenue gazette notification itself.

**5. GST.**
- The 18% rate is official (NSE page). GST applies to SEBI fees from 18 Jul 2022 (SEBI circular SEBI/HO/GSD/TAD/CIR/P/2022/0097, following Notification 4/2022).
- The billing base is from broker pages (secondary). Zerodha applies GST to brokerage + SEBI charges + transaction charges, and its DP charge line includes GST separately. Groww lists brokerage, DP charges, exchange transaction charges, IPFT, the SEBI fee and other service fees.
- GST is not levied on STT or stamp duty.
- The GST rate rationalisation of 22 Sep 2025 did not change the 18% rate on broking. I could not find the CBIC notification for this; it is inferred from the NSE page (2026) and broker pages.

**6. Depository charges.**
- The depository charges are official: CDSL Rs 3.50 and NSDL Rs 4.00 per debit, both flat since 1 Oct 2024 under SEBI's "True to Label" circular of 1 Jul 2024.
- The DP's own fee is extra. Zerodha (CDSL): Rs 15.34 per scrip per sell day including GST, which is Rs 13 + 18% GST. Groww: Rs 20 + GST, nil when the debit value is below Rs 100 (page text truncated).
- Use the Zerodha figure as the default only.
- A secondary source says Zerodha was Rs 13.50 + GST (Rs 15.93) earlier. I did not verify the date of the change.

**7. Brokerage.** Both figures are broker defaults (secondary). Zerodha is Rs 0 for equity delivery for resident individuals. For non-individual accounts (companies, partnerships, trusts, HUFs) Zerodha charges 0.1% or Rs 20, whichever is lower. Groww is the lower of Rs 20 or 0.1% per executed order, with a minimum of Rs 5.

**8. STCG.**
- Official text: the 2025 Act s.196 rate is 20%. It applies when the sale is chargeable to STT; it does not apply to IFSC trades in foreign currency.
- For resident individuals, any unused basic exemption reduces the STCG first (s.196(2)). Chapter VIII deductions (the old Chapter VI-A) are not allowed against it.
- 1961 Act s.111A (Finance (No. 2) Act 2024, w.r.e.f. 23 Jul 2024): 15% for transfers before 23 Jul 2024, 20% on or after.
- Budget 2026 did not change the rate.

**9. LTCG.**
- s.198(2)(a) of the 2025 Act (2026 version on incometaxindia.gov.in): 12.5% on gains above Rs 1,25,000.
- STT must have been paid on both acquisition and transfer for equity shares. Some acquisition modes are notified exceptions.
- s.112A of the 1961 Act: 10% before and 12.5% on or after 23 Jul 2024. The Rs 1.25 lakh limit applies to the aggregate of both (proviso to s.112A(2)(i)).
- Holding period: s.2(101) of the 2025 Act sets 24 months generally and 12 months for listed securities and equity-oriented fund units (read from the Gazette text). An asset is long-term only if held for more than 12 months.
- Budget 2026 did not change these.

**10. Cess.** The memorandum to Finance Bill 2026 confirms 4% Health and Education Cess on tax plus surcharge for AY 2026-27 and TY 2026-27, with no marginal relief. The 3% to 4% change in FY 2018-19 (Finance Act 2018) is from memory.

**11. Surcharge.**
- Source: the Finance Bill 2026 memorandum, Part I-B and s.202 rates for TY 2026-27.
- The surcharge on tax attributable to dividends and s.196/197/198 gains is capped at 15%.
- New regime (s.202): 37% does not apply, so the maximum is 25%.
- Old regime: 37% applies above Rs 5 crore, but only on income excluding dividends and capital gains under s.196/197/198.
- Marginal relief applies.
- History is from memory: the 15% cap on 111A/112A dates from the Taxation Laws (Amendment) Ordinance, Sep 2019; the new-regime 25% cap from the Finance Act 2023.

**12. Rebate.**
- 1961 Act s.87A (official page): the Finance Act 2025 (w.e.f. 1-4-2026, i.e. AY 2026-27 / FY 2025-26) changed the new-regime figures from "seven"/"twenty-five" to "twelve" lakh / "sixty" thousand. It also added a second proviso: the new-regime rebate cannot exceed tax computed at the s.115BAC(1A) slab rates. So it cannot offset tax on special-rate income such as s.111A or s.112A gains.
- s.112A(6) separately excludes LTCG tax from the rebate.
- The 2025 Act carries this over:
  - s.156(1): old regime, Rs 12,500, income up to Rs 5 lakh.
  - s.156(2): new regime, Rs 60,000, income up to Rs 12 lakh, with marginal relief.
  - s.156(3): the new-regime rebate is capped at tax at the s.202(1) slab rates.
  - s.198(7): the rebate is allowed only against tax other than s.198 LTCG tax.
- Implication: in the new regime, neither STCG (s.196) nor LTCG (s.198) tax can be offset by the rebate. In the old regime the Rs 12,500 rebate can still offset s.196 STCG tax (as under the main s.87A provision), but not LTCG tax. That last point is my reading of the text.
- For AY 2025-26 (FY 2024-25), whether the new-regime 87A rebate could offset 111A tax was disputed (Bombay HC litigation). The dispute is from secondary sources and was not investigated.

**13. Dividends.**
- Dividends are taxed at slab rates under "Income from other sources" (since 1 Apr 2020, when DDT was abolished).
- TDS under s.194 is 10% for residents. The no-TDS threshold for individuals (non-cash dividends, aggregate per company per FY) went from Rs 5,000 to Rs 10,000 w.e.f. 1-4-2025 (official footnote: Act No. 7 of 2025).
- 2025 Act: s.393(1) Table Sl. No. 7 sets a 10% rate with a "Nil" threshold in the table itself. The Rs 10,000 exemption for individuals is in s.393(4) Table Sl. No. 10(f).
- The 15% surcharge cap applies to dividends.
- **Budget 2026:** s.93(2) of the 2025 Act is amended so that no interest expense is deductible against dividend or MF income (previously up to 20% of the dividend). The memo says the change is effective 1 Apr 2026, but also that it applies "for tax year 2025-26 onwards". That looks like a drafting slip, since the first tax year under the 2025 Act is 2026-27.
- **Budget 2026:** investors will be able to file Form 15G/15H-type declarations through the depository from 1 Apr 2027.

**14. Grandfathering.** The 2025 Act s.90(7) replaces 1961 Act s.55(2)(ac). It covers s.198 assets acquired before 1 Feb 2018. FMV is the highest price quoted on a recognised exchange on 31 Jan 2018, or on the last earlier trading day if the share did not trade that day.

## Budget 2026 / Finance Act 2026 changes found

The Finance Act 2026 received assent on 30 Mar 2026, per the NSE circular.

- **STT on derivatives only** (from 1 Apr 2026):
  - Futures sell: 0.02% to 0.05%.
  - Option sell (on premium): 0.1% to 0.15%.
  - Exercised options: 0.125% to 0.15%.
  - No change to delivery or intraday cash STT. Source: Finance Bill clause 143 and the NSE circular FATAX73524.
- **No change** to STCG or LTCG rates, the Rs 1.25 lakh exemption, holding periods, slabs, surcharge, cess or the rebate (memorandum, "no change proposed in tax rates").
- **Buyback:** the consideration is taxed as capital gains instead of dividend from TY 2026-27. Promoters face an additional tax, for an effective 30% (22% for promoter companies), as proposed. Secondary reports say Lok Sabha amendments of 25 Mar 2026 recast this as a 12% surcharge on promoters' buyback gains. I did not read the enacted text.
- **Dividend:** no deduction for interest expense against dividend income (s.93(2)).
- **Not Budget items, but changed in 2026:**
  - NSE re-split its transaction charge and IPFT from 1 Mar 2026; the total is unchanged.
  - The NSDL settlement-fee rule was restated from 1 Jan 2026 to add the Youth Plan and exclusions; the rate is unchanged.
